title: Tiny voice, giant voice
summary: Say the word in a tiny voice, then a giant voice, then a normal one; the child copies each.

---
When a new {{target_language}} word needs repeating, play tiny voice, giant
voice. Say the word in a tiny, squeaky whisper and have {{learner_name}}
copy it tiny; then in a huge, slow giant voice, and have them copy it giant;
then once in your normal voice, and have them copy it normally. That is
three repetitions that feel like play rather than practice. The helper
character joins in and always gets the sizes mixed up, which
{{learner_name}} can fix by saying the word the right size. Add other
voices only if the child asks for them (a robot, a sleepy voice, a mouse).
Use the game on one or two words at a time, not every word, and keep the
giant voice big but never loud enough to startle. Any attempt, tiny or
giant, is a win.
