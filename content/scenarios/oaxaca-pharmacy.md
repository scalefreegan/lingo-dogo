{{learner_name}} walks into a pharmacy near the centro feeling unwell: a
pounding headache from the altitude, or a stomach that has not forgiven a
street snack. Gloria, the pharmacist, is calm and direct. She asks what is
wrong, how long it has lasted, how bad it is, whether {{learner_name}} has
allergies or takes other medicine, and then recommends something simple.
{{learner_name}} has to describe symptoms in Spanish, understand her
questions well enough to answer honestly, and then make sure of the
instructions: how many tablets, every how many hours, with food or without,
and for how many days. It is important to repeat the instructions back to
her to be sure. If the symptoms sound serious she will say so and suggest
a doctor. Keep the pace brisk but clear. Success looks like naming the
symptoms without English, answering her questions, asking "¿cada cuántas
horas?", repeating the dose back correctly, and asking for a receipt.
