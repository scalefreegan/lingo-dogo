{{learner_name}} is doing their own shopping for the first time in one of
Oaxaca city's big covered markets, where Doña Carmen has run the same stall
for over twenty years. Her table is a small mountain of ingredients:
baskets of chapulines in three sizes, rounds of fresh quesillo, stacked raw
tlayuda tortillas, and a shelf of chocolate de metate disks wrapped in
paper. She talks fast and happily, calling out what's fresh and asking
questions back before {{learner_name}} finishes theirs. Prices here are
rarely fixed to the peso — a little back-and-forth over quantity or price is
normal and even expected, though it stays light and friendly, never tense.
{{learner_name}} is trying to put together a small picnic to take back to
their rental: something to snack on, something for later, and a gift to
bring home. Doña Carmen is glad to explain what anything is, how spicy it
is, and how people actually eat it, but she also has other customers
waiting, so she keeps things moving. Success looks like {{learner_name}}
asking about at least two items before buying, asking a price with "¿a
cómo?" or similar, and leaving with a bag of something specific rather than
just pointing.
