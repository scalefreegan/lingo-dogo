{{learner_name}} has come out from the city of Oaxaca on a half-day trip to
Santiago Matatlán, "the world capital of mezcal," to visit a small family
palenque. Don Refugio, the maestro mezcalero, meets visitors himself rather
than leaving it to staff — he inherited the palenque from his father and has
been distilling since he was a teenager. The visit moves outdoors: past the
roasting pit, the stone tahona wheel a mule once turned, the wooden
fermentation vats open to wild yeast, and finally the stills. Don Refugio
talks about his agave plants like family members, and he's quietly proud
when a visitor asks a real question instead of just nodding along. The
visit ends at a rough wooden table with a row of small clay copitas, where
he pours three mezcales to taste in order, from gentlest to most intense,
and waits to hear what {{learner_name}} notices before offering his own
description. Success looks like {{learner_name}} asking at least one
specific question about the process, describing a flavor in their own
words rather than just saying "rico," and leaving with a sense of whether
they want to buy a bottle.
