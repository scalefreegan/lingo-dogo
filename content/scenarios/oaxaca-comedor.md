{{learner_name}} has walked into a small comedor a few blocks from the
Zócalo — no sign to speak of, four tables, run out of Señora Lupe's front
room with her daughter helping in the kitchen. There's no printed menu;
Señora Lupe simply tells each table what she cooked today, usually a soup, a
main with a choice of one or two moles or guisados, rice, and a drink, all
for one set price. She's warm but direct, the way someone is when they've
fed the same neighbors for twenty years, and she'll happily describe what's
in anything if asked, partly out of pride in the recipe. {{learner_name}}
has a question to settle before ordering — maybe spice level, maybe a
dietary restriction — and needs Señora Lupe's help finding the right dish
rather than just pointing at an item. The comedor is busy but not rushed;
conversation at the table is normal while the food comes out. Success looks
like {{learner_name}} asking what's in at least one dish before ordering
it, stating any restriction clearly, and asking for the check in a natural
way at the end rather than just standing up.
