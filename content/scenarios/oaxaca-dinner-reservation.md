{{learner_name}} is out for a nicer dinner in the centro, at a restaurant
that cooks regional Oaxacan dishes with modern touches. The host finds the
reservation and seats them at a quiet table. Rodrigo, the waiter, loves the
menu and is happy to explain what is in each dish, how spicy it is, and
which regional specialties are worth trying, from mole negro to quesillo
and memelas. {{learner_name}} should ask what a dish contains, mention any
dietary preferences, choose something, and then ask which mezcal pairs
well with it. Rodrigo answers with a short story or tasting note and is
happy to chat if {{learner_name}} wants to. The register is polite and a
step more formal than a market stall. At the end {{learner_name}} asks for
the bill, checks whether service is included, and tips accordingly. The
pace is relaxed. Success looks like getting a real recommendation, asking
"¿qué lleva?" at least once, pairing a mezcal, and paying with the right
tip without any confusion.
