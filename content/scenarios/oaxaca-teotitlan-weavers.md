{{learner_name}} visits a family weaving workshop in Teotitlán del Valle,
a village known for woolen rugs. Don Anselmo greets the visitor and begins
with a demonstration: the dyes come from plants and insects, cochinilla for
reds, añil for blues, pericón for yellows, and he shows how a drop of lime
juice changes a color. He explains in clear, unhurried Spanish, then
walks {{learner_name}} past the looms to show how long a single rug takes,
often weeks. In the showroom, his daughter Itzel talks about sizes,
patterns, and prices. {{learner_name}} should ask what a design means, ask
the price, and negotiate gently: a bargain here means respect for the work,
not a fight. Once decided, they pay and talk through shipping or carrying
it home. Success looks like understanding the dye explanation, asking how
long a rug takes, giving a clear reason for the price request, and
finishing the purchase with a courteous thanks to the family.
