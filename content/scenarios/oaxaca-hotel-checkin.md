{{learner_name}} arrives at a small colonial hotel in the centro histórico
and walks up to the front desk. Sra. Ofelia is courteous, efficient, and
speaks at a natural pace, with a little more care when she notices
{{learner_name}} struggling. The first part is check-in: the reservation
name, a passport, how many nights. Then come the practical questions a
traveler really asks: what time breakfast is served, the wifi password,
whether the tap water is safe to drink, where to get purified water, and
whether a late checkout is possible on the last day. After seeing the room,
{{learner_name}} finds one small problem, such as no hot water or a noisy
window, and has to explain it politely and ask for a solution. The tone is
calm and helpful. Success looks like completing check-in without English,
asking at least three practical questions in full sentences, describing
the room problem clearly enough that Sra. Ofelia knows what to fix, and
confirming the checkout time.
