{{learner_name}} comes across a street stand near the centro late in the
evening, where smoke from the charcoal rises past a line of hungry locals.
Doña Tere runs the comal and calls out orders like a drill sergeant,
though she is quick to joke. The menu is short: tlayudas and memelas, with
tasajo, cecina, or chorizo. {{learner_name}} needs to decide quickly, say
what they want, and answer her questions about toppings and spice. There
are no chairs, so everyone eats standing, plates in hand, and the
conversation is loud and informal. A regular customer strikes up a chat
and asks where {{learner_name}} is from. Speech here is fast, clipped, and
full of local slang, so the dojo master should keep sentences short and
repeat if asked. Success looks like ordering with confidence, saying how
spicy to make it, joining the small talk with the other customer, and
paying and saying a warm goodnight.
