{{learner_name}} drops into a small specialty café a few blocks from the
zócalo, the kind of place with a handwritten menu and a long counter.
Mariana, the barista, is easygoing and serious about her coffee, and is
glad to explain what beans she is using and where they grow. The learner
orders a pour-over, asks about the origin, picks one, and asks what flavor
to expect. Then comes the practical part: the wifi password, a table, and
whether it is okay to stay for an hour. When Mariana brings the cup over
she stays to chat, asking where the learner is from and what they have
seen, and then telling them about the neighborhood, where to walk, where
to eat. This is a low-pressure scene, so the conversation can wander.
Success looks like ordering without pointing, describing the taste in at
least a short sentence, asking a follow-up question about the neighborhood,
and paying and leaving with a recommendation.
